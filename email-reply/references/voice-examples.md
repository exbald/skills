## Format

One `## Example N - <situation>` section per email. Quote the email (or the
telling part of it), then add a short `Notice:` line naming what the example
demonstrates - that annotation is what teaches the pattern, not just the text.

Capture at least:
- a quick acknowledgment / short reply
- a longer explanation or proposal-style email
- an awkward one (pushback, bad news, a "no", a price)

## Example 1 - short factual reply (FICTIONAL - replace with yours)

> Hi Sam,
> The link gives me a "document not found" error - could you double-check the
> sharing settings on your end?
>
> Also, quick heads-up on invoicing: anything we kick off this month lands on
> the April invoice, so if budget timing matters, say the word and I'll shift
> the start date.

Notice: states the problem plainly, asks one clear question, delivers an
awkward money point without apology or padding.

## Example 2 - acknowledging a new request (FICTIONAL - replace with yours)

> Hi Sam,
>
> Happy to take this on! I'll read the brief properly over the weekend and come
> back early next week with my take, timeline, and pricing.
>
> One quick question while I work through it: when you say the team should be
> able to edit reports themselves, are you picturing saved views (pick columns
> and filters on the data that's already there) or a full drag-and-drop report
> builder? Big difference in build size, so I want to price the right one.
>
> Thanks,
> Alex

Notice: sets explicit expectations (when the reply comes, what it contains).
The single question carries its own justification, and it plants the scope
boundary before the quote conversation starts.
